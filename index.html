📖 Quick guide

How a hand works

Two forced bets (small blind $5, big blind $10) start the pot. Everyone gets 2 private cards, then there are up to four betting rounds: preflop → flop (3 shared cards) → turn (1 more) → river (1 more). Best 5-card hand from your 2 + the 5 shared cards wins. You can also win by making everyone fold.

Hand rankings (best → worst)

  1. Royal / Straight Flush — five in a row, same suit
  2. Four of a Kind — e.g. 9 9 9 9
  3. Full House — three of a kind + a pair
  4. Flush — five of the same suit
  5. Straight — five in a row (A can be high or low)
  6. Three of a Kind
  7. Two Pair
  8. One Pair
  9. High Card

Position is power

The dealer button (BTN) acts last after the flop — you see what everyone does before deciding. That's why you can play more hands from the button/cutoff (CO) and should play very tight from under the gun (UTG) and the blinds. The seat labels on the table (BTN, SB, BB, UTG, HJ, CO) update every hand.

Pot odds in 20 seconds

If you must call $20 into an $80 pot, you're paying 20 to win 100 → you need to win 20% of the time to break even. Compare that to your chance of winning (the coach estimates it for you). Rule of thumb for draws: outs × 4 ≈ your % on the flop (two cards to come), outs × 2 on the turn.

Know your opponents

  • 🪨 Rocky (tight & careful) — barely plays a hand. When he bets big, fold unless you're huge. Steal his blinds.
  • 🦈 Tanya (tight-aggressive) — the dangerous one. Solid hands, played hard. Avoid marginal spots against her.
  • 🃏 Loco (loose & wild) — raises with anything. Call down lighter, let him bluff into you, don't try to out-bluff him.
  • 📞 Callie (calling station) — never folds. Never bluff her. Value-bet even medium hands relentlessly.
  • 🎩 Prof (solid all-round) — balanced. Play straightforward poker.

Beginner rules of thumb

  • Fold most hands preflop. Tight is right while learning.
  • Enter pots with a raise, not a limp (call).
  • Bet your strong hands — don't "trap" as a habit; free cards beat you.
  • When a tight player raises you back, believe them.
  • Judge your decisions, not the result of one hand. Good decisions win over time.

Glossary
